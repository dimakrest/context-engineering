Session-grounded retrospective · revised 6 September 2026 · three mission runs

Keep the review discipline.
Make execution durable.

The most valuable thing to borrow from MCMV is executable control over worker completion, recovery, and evidence—not more instructions telling an agent to work autonomously. The third run proved it: the prose rule that fixed “stop at a passed milestone” did nothing for “wait forever on a child that already finished.”

What to take from MCMV—and the improvement from each

1 · Highest priority · Take the mechanism (was 3)

One completion signal keyed by task, validated once, with a watchdog

Session evidence. The handoff hook fires the instant a background dispatch returns, before the worker has started. In S3 that was every one of 19 worker dispatches; the orchestrator learned to ignore it, then also ignored three real “no handoff” situations. Separately, S1’s orchestrator misread a valid Markdown exit-code column. S1:839, 884, 907; J3:177–178; N:1–2.

Take from MCMV. The driver waits for the child process before grading. Worker-facing validation and the driver use the same handoff_verdict; local commit evidence rejects a worker merely reporting its inherited base commit. M:1019–1123, 964–1011, 1622–1625, 2240–2260.

Improvement. Dispatch acknowledgement cannot be misclassified as completion. A commit on the branch with no handoff is detected by the loop, not by a human. Workers can run the same check they are graded against.

Bell adaptation. mission-journal.sh already reads tool_response.status and distinguishes async_launched; mission-handoff-schema.sh reads only tool_input. Gate the check on the response status, then add the second half: a watchdog that treats (commit on the mission branch) ∨ (idle notification) ∨ (N minutes of silence) as “verify now”. F015 is the proof of shape: the orchestrator armed a Monitor on the commit by hand and the loop closed itself in 4 minutes. J3:97–98; hooks/mission-journal.sh:7–13,36,62; V:12–39.

Proof it is worth it

  • 10 h 17 min of S3 stalls — F003 (8 min), F012 (44 min), F014 (10 min), the code-review fork (9 h 15) — all ended by a human question; none by the system.
  • 29 false alarms from the hook meant to catch exactly this; N:1: “a hook that is always wrong trains the orchestrator to ignore a hook that is sometimes right.”
  • F012’s idle message — “I'll pause here and wait for the background commit to complete” — arrived at 15:20:35Z; its commit 5586689e2 had landed at 15:19:58Z. The orchestrator: “I took the agent's word for its own state instead of checking it.” S3:4667, 4726, 4730.
  • F015, with a Monitor armed: commit 04:17:00Z → commit_observed 04:21:22Z → handoff_ingested 04:21:29Z. Same failure class, zero human involvement. J3:97–98.

2 · Highest priority · Adapt (was 1)

An executable runner for the entire authorised pipeline

Session evidence. S1: after M2 and again after M3 the turn ended on a status summary — “the loop shouldn't have stopped”, “no reason to stop”. S3: the milestone stops did not recur, but the orchestrator launched /code-review as a forked background skill, whose ten finder agents reported to the orchestrator’s mailbox instead of the fork’s; the fork’s last words were “Now waiting on the 10 finder agents” and it never woke. S1:1212–1223, 1356–1361; S3:6645–6691; agent-abd4eb9a:89; N:8.

Take from MCMV. cmd_run selects pending work in a program loop, waits for a worker, evaluates its result, and advances or records a specific stop reason. Progress does not rely on the conversational orchestrator deciding to take another turn. M:2023–2150, 2240–2260, 1990–2005.

Improvement. A passed milestone, an ingested handoff, or a finished review schedules the next authorised step. A status update is not an implicit end of execution. Nothing is awaited without a deadline.

Bell adaptation. The 0.2.0 “ADVISORY proceeds” text (B:224–247) already covers milestone boundaries and the evidence says it works. What it cannot cover is a turn that ends while a child is running, or a fork whose children report elsewhere. Extend the runner to validation, PR-review and forked skills: every background launch gets a deadline and a fallback (“assess the finder outputs directly”), which is precisely what the human triggered at 03:59Z. Keep independent read-only reviews parallel; keep writing ownership serial. Do not import MCMV’s mandatory G2 approval at every milestone. Never auto-merge.

Proof it is worth it

  • S1: 3 h 08 min (14:27Z → 17:35Z) and 5 min after passed milestones; both ended with “why did you stop?”. S1:1212–1213, 1356–1357.
  • S3: 9 h 15 min (18:44Z Sep 2 → 03:59Z Sep 3) on a fork that could never resume; recovery took the orchestrator 3 minutes once asked — it spawned six assessors and finished the review the same morning. S3:6669–6691; J3:87–92.
  • The plugin’s own SKILL.md records the pre-plugin baseline: “idle for ~85% of its nine days, 13 of its 15 long waits ended with the human typing continue.” S3’s journal-metrics still reports 79 % idle over 61 h. B:224–225; journal-metrics.
  • Milestone-class stops: 2 in S1, 0 in S3 — the one class a prose rule could fix, it fixed. The remaining stalls are structural.

3 · Highest priority · Adapt + extend (was 2)

Typed worker outcomes and reconciliation before retry

Session evidence. S2’s fixer reported idleReason: failed with a session-limit reason; the parent hit the same limit; the reset came at 21:00Z and nothing used it. Next morning the parent verified the already-written work rather than starting from zero — by hand. S3: F010’s worker ran git checkout -- AnalyticsFilters.tsx to undo a mutation and wiped its own uncommitted edits with it; F012 self-reported a state 37 s stale. S2:17204–17211; J3:26, 59; S3:4730.

Take from MCMV. Worker execution records exit, elapsed time, cost, timeout and the CLI error detail; infrastructure errors are classified apart from substantive failures. cmd_resume checks handoffs and commit ancestry before deciding to merge, mark done or requeue. inherited_work_note tells a new worker about interrupted changes. M:1620–1700, 2485–2555, 1290–1319.

Improvement. “No handoff” no longer means “the worker did nothing.” Quota, malformed handoff, failed test and still-running are four different recovery actions. Completed edits survive a lost reporting turn; retries do not duplicate them; a worker’s self-report is never the source of truth for its state.

Bell adaptation. Provider-neutral task IDs, process/session ownership, a tree snapshot at every idle (a patch under .missions/<slug>/partial/), and a generated recovery packet. A quota outcome carries resume_at; waking at that time needs an external scheduler, which a stopped process cannot be. Claude-to-Codex continuation is an extension, not an MCMV capability. Provider switch and extra spend need prior authorisation.

Proof it is worth it

  • S2: fixer failed 19:54Z, limit reset 21:00Z, human 03:59Z — 7 h of recoverable time with a dirty tree that only a human inspection recovered. S2:17204–17211.
  • S3 F010: “a mid-work git checkout -- wiped its own uncommitted (a)+(c) edits along with the mutation; it re-applied and verified.” Recovered by luck of a diligent worker; a snapshot would make it recoverable by rule. J3:26.
  • S3 F012 / F003 / F014: three stale or absent self-reports; every one resolved by git log + ls handoffs/ once someone ran them. The orchestrator wrote the lesson itself: “verify state rather than trust the agent's prose status.” N:2; S3:4730.
  • S3 M2 scrutiny: the validator’s own caveat (“ran in parallel, contention possible”) is what turned five red results into a non-event. Typed outcomes make that caveat a field, not a paragraph. J3:45–46.

4 · High priority · Extend the existing test suite (was 4)

End-to-end tests and mutation tests for the workflow itself

Session evidence. The background false alarm was first reported on 20 August (S1:907) and fired again on 2 September in a plugin two versions newer. Take from MCMV. Its regression harness creates temporary repositories, substitutes a stub CLI and exercises the real driver. A separate mutation harness deliberately breaks the driver and checks that tests notice. E:1–36; U:1–15.

5 · High priority · Take the evidence model (was 6)

Assertions that name the observation and the defect that must fail

Session evidence. S1: removing tenant filters left a suite green; a fixture gave assignment and reply one timestamp, so a KPI was 41× flattering; a ranking symptom returned through three fixes. S3: F001 claimed eight assertions on 9,319 green tests; blind review deleted the predicate wiring and 92/92 stayed green. The F003 worker “drafted the A013 mutation claim before running it, then actually ran it.” S1:1042, 2164, 1547; J3:185, 207, 213, 18.

Take from MCMV. Preflight requires contract evidence metadata naming an observation surface and a falsifying change. Opt-in fixture coverage maps behavioural assertions to a fault fixture, a catching check and an evidence pointer. M:609–619, 677–694.

Improvement. “Tests passed” becomes “this test distinguishes the required behaviour from the realistic wrong implementation” — and the worker proves it before the reviewer has to. Repair rounds shrink because the first handoff already carries the mutation the reviewer would have run.

Bell adaptation. The S3 contract already went three cross-check rounds and still shipped assertions whose tests were inert. Add an executable falsify: field per assertion, require a red-then-green row for it in the handoff, and have the schema hook reject a claimed assertion without one. Keep blind review — its job becomes finding the mutation the contract did not name, not re-running the one it did. Split proofs need explicit ownership (one final owner with contributors) — MCMV’s exactly-one-owner rule needs that adaptation.

Proof it is worth it

  • S3: 9 of 14 features were repairs or review-driven; each repair is a worker plus a blind review — roughly 18 of 36 dispatches and, by the journal timestamps, 10–25 min each. J3:221–226, 3–9, 18–37, 71–76.
  • 6 S3 defects and 5 S1 defects were invisible to a green suite and visible to one mutation. Four of the S3 six were test-side — the product code was right, the proof was not — which is precisely what a named falsifier prevents. J3:207, 213; N:6.
  • Where the contract did name the mutation (A002 DST cases, A013 cache invalidation), workers ran it themselves and reviewers confirmed on first pass. J3:132, 18, 21.

6 · Medium-high priority · Adapt (was 5)

A verified command and environment manifest

Session evidence. S1: several worktrees computed the same Compose project name, so a documented docker compose exec would test another checkout’s source. S3: the M2 scrutiny validator ran seven suites in parallel and reported 1 pytest + 4 vitest failures and a pyright OOM; all five passed in isolation, and the repo’s pyright gate turned out to run on changed files only. Three pre-existing tooling gaps (missing beautifulsoup4, make bootstrap failing on core.hooksPath, e2e deps unprovisioned) each cost a worker a detour. S1:907; J3:45–48, 179, 41–42.

Take from MCMV. services.yaml centralises build/test/lint/service commands and tells planning to verify them before use; preflight checks that key commands are declared. Y:1–42; M:790–794.

Improvement. Every worker and validator receives the same verified recipe, including how suites may be combined. A test result carries the identity of the checkout and services it exercised. Environment failures stop being misdiagnosed as code defects — or, as in S3, spending 30 minutes to prove they are not.

What not to import wholesale

  • Mandatory human approval after every green milestone. MCMV raises G2 gates inside the runner; S3 shows the advisory ceiling works when the runtime does. Borrow gate mechanics, not its fixed approval policy. M:2037–2047, 2140–2147.

  • Worker configuration stripping. MCMV removes inherited hooks and permissions, then reinstalls selected guards. Do not remove Bell’s safety checks to gain a headless runner. M:1570–1608.
  • An agent-readable approval secret as a security boundary. MCMV’s approval path checks environment/TTY state and has a test bypass. Use the trusted host approval mechanism. M:2687–2726.
